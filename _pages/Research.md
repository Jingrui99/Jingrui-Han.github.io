# PhD Project: Human-AI Co-Creation in Film Music
**A Practice-Based Study of Collaborative Scoring**

---

## 📂 TASK ONE: Keywords & Research Concepts
*Developed through library database searching and initial conceptual mapping.*

### Core Keywords
*   **Relational Agency:** Moving beyond "AI as a tool" to "AI as a non-human collaborator." I focus on how AI and composers co-create in real-time performance through **Gestural Interaction**.
*   **Collaborative Workflow:** Current AI music software often operates under "Single Agency," which is insufficient for the complex, image-synced demands of film scoring. My research explores a new workflow allowing for real-time feedback and joint decision-making.
*   **Embodied Composition:** Investigating how composers align emotionally with AI through bodily senses and physical gestures rather than mere code or MIDI input.

### New Concepts (Kupu/Key Words)
*   **Whakapapa of Sound:** Exploring the genealogy, lineage, and cultural origins of digital samples and training data.
*   **Narrative-Aware AI:** An intelligent entity capable of perceiving the rhythm of cinematic imagery and synchronizing with the composer’s intent in real-time.

## 📂 TASK TWO: The Materials of Life (Object Study)
*Reflecting on Tim Ingold’s "Making: Anthropology, Archaeology, Art and Architecture" (2013).*

**The Object: Headphones**

*   **Environmental Matter:** Headphones are not isolated objects. They encompass the hum of electricity, filtered ambient noise, and the tactile pressure exerted during wear.
*   **Academic Reflection:** 
    At first glance, headphones are a technical tool. However, in my sound practice, they function as an **"Environment."** Through them, I do not just "listen" to sound—I **"inhabit"** a specific acoustic space. They filter, compress, isolate, and sometimes deceive; they shape the very way we perceive the world.

    Ingold argues that materials are not static objects but processes—flows of matter in formation. Digital sound works the same way: it is not a fixed entity but is constantly changing through software, electricity, and the listening body.

    > **"I do not merely produce music; I enter a state of co-existence with sound through the environment of the headphones."**

## 📂 TASK THREE: Indigenous Epistemology & Methodology
*A Response to Manulani Aluli Meyer’s “Indigenous and Authentic.”*

### Methodological Reflection
Aluli Meyer emphasizes the "Triangulation of Meaning" through **Body, Mind, and Spirit**. This framework deeply challenges the current disembodied nature of human-AI interaction.

*   **My Research Question:** "If Indigenous knowledge is inherently embodied, then existing 'Single Agency' AI tools feel hollow due to their lack of sensory presence. Can we re-introduce the 'Body' into digital creation through **gestural interaction** and **headphone immersion**, thereby breathing a 'Technological Spirit' into AI collaboration?"

*   **Cultural Politics & Practice:** Meyer argues that methodology is personal. For my research, this means refusing to treat AI as a cold "black box." I am attempting to build a **relationship of mutual respect**—where the AI generates the infinite possibilities of latent space, and my body (through gesture and listening) provides the narrative truth of the film.

### New Method Experiment
*   **"Gestural Mapping Experiment":** I am using camera-based motion tracking to capture my conducting gestures as I watch film clips. The acceleration of my hands is mapped to the "emotional density" of the AI’s audio generation, replacing traditional keyboard/mouse inputs.
*   **Observation:** This method breaks the linear "input-output" model, creating a state of "embodied cognition" similar to the one described by Aluli Meyer.

## 📂 Week 3: Self-Directed Research Questions
*Quick-fire generation of inquiries for the collaborative scoring practice:*

1.  When gesture becomes the language of communication, can AI perceive the "emotional tension" in a composer’s movement?
2.  How can we break the "Single Agency" of AI software to allow it to truly understand cinematic narrative logic?
3.  Can AI understand the "silence" between notes in a film, or does it only perceive the data of the notes themselves?
4.  How does the "fluidity" of digital sound challenge the traditional concept of a "fixed version" in film scoring?
5.  How does the "Black Box" nature of algorithms affect the composer’s sense of creative agency?
6.  Can gestural interaction effectively bridge the gap in AI’s lack of "spiritual" expression?
7.  Does AI-generated music carry a form of "Technological Whakapapa" (lineage)?
8.  How can a film director participate in this three-way dialogue between human, machine, and image?
9.  If sound is a "process," how should we define the point of completion in a collaborative score?
10. **(Selected Question): At the intersection of gesture and algorithm, who is truly leading the emotional narrative of the film?**
